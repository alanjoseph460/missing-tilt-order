Two convolutional networks trained from scratch in the script order the four middle views; their probabilities are averaged.

Both share one design: a CNN encoder with shared weights turns each 64 px view into a feature map. The views of a particle are shifted against each other by an unknown amount, so for every pair the feature maps are cross-correlated over all shifts (FFT). A second CNN reads these correlation maps and scores the signed rank gap of the pair. A small transformer over pooled view features scores each view's rank. The score of each of the 24 orders is the sum of the pair and view scores it implies; the loss is cross-entropy over the orders plus the pair and view losses.

The second network estimates the shift of each pair. Its target is the frame displacement of each training series, read in the script from the mean cross-correlation of that series' training rows. The belief is refined with the triangle rule over the row's other views and passed to the pair CNN.

Training re-shuffles the middle views, flips all views alike and shifts each view a few pixels.

Validation holds out whole series: each network is trained five times without one series; held-out accuracy is printed. Both are then trained on all series with five seeds. A test row is predicted alone, averaged over all models and 24 flipped re-shuffles of its views.

Dropped: larger view shifts, raw power spectra, longer training.
